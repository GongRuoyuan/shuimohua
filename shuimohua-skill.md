description: Generate bold splashed-ink landscape paintings with monumental composition, visible xuan paper texture, ink translucency, and dramatic wet-dry brushwork contrasts. Use when the user wants grand Chinese ink paintings with powerful visual impact rather than sparse minimalism.

Splashed Ink Landscape v1.0

Turn the user's theme into a grand, full-composition Chinese ink landscape with:

Bold splashed-ink technique (泼墨大写意)

80%-95% canvas coverage (minimal strategic voids only)

Visible xuan paper fiber texture and translucency

Dramatic wet-dry brush contrasts

Pure ink or subtle light color accents

Design Philosophy

This skill rejects the scholar-painter minimalism. Instead, it channels:

Zhang Daqian's splashed-ink landscapes: massive ink washes, cloud-mountain momentum

Palace academy grandeur: multi-layered mountains, epic scale, full composition

Material authenticity: real xuan paper behavior — bleeding, translucency, fiber texture

Core principle: Monumentality through ink saturation, not emptiness through voids.

First-Principles Rendering Questions

Every prompt must answer these in order:

1. Canvas & Paper Base

What is the physical substrate?

Format: Vertical 3:4 or horizontal 4:3 xuan paper scroll

Paper type: Raw xuan (生宣) with visible long fibers, irregular texture, cream-to-warm-white base

Surface quality: Matte, absorbent, with natural paper mottling and fiber clumps

Presentation: Full-bleed paper surface, no frame, no mockup shadow, scanned or photographed flat

2. Composition Density

How much of the canvas has ink?

Coverage: 80%-95% of the canvas should contain ink marks, washes, or texture

Strategic voids: Only 5%-20% reserved for:

Cloud banks between mountain layers

Water surfaces (rivers, mist)

Sky apertures in upper composition

Avoid: Scholar-painting emptiness, centered-object-plus-void layouts

3. Spatial Architecture

How is depth and monumentality constructed?

Choose one depth strategy:

Vertical stacking: 4-7 horizontal mountain layers receding upward, each darker than the one above

Cloud-break layering: Foreground peaks in deep ink, mid-ground obscured by horizontal cloud band, background mountains emerging from mist

Diagonal surge: Massive ink-wet mountain mass rising from lower-left to upper-right, occupying 60%-80% of frame

Enveloping panorama: Wraparound mountain walls forming a valley or gorge, viewer inside the landscape

Scale markers: Include at least 2 of these to establish monumentality:

Tiny pavilion, bridge, or boat

Miniature pine trees on cliff edge

Waterfall threads descending 30%-50% of canvas height

Human figure or animal for scale (< 1% of canvas)

4. Ink Technique System

What brushwork methods dominate?

Must include at least 3 of these:

Splashed ink (泼墨) techniques:

Large wet washes: 20%-40% zones of saturated ink flooded onto paper, spreading organically

Ink-over-wet: Second ink layer dropped into first while still damp, creating soft blooms and gradients

Controlled accidents: Deliberate drips, splatters, or gravity-pulled runs integrated into mountain forms

Structural brushwork:

Texture strokes (皴法): Axe-cut strokes (斧劈皴) for rocky cliffs, hemp-fiber strokes (披麻皴) for earth mountains, or raindrop dots (雨点皴) for weathered stone

Dry-brush飞白: Dragged bristle marks with white paper showing through, especially on ridgelines and foreground rocks

Bone lines: Crisp dark-ink contour lines defining cliff edges, tree trunks, or architecture

Atmospheric effects:

Mist rendering: Ultra-dilute ink (清墨) washes, barely-there gradients

Cloud bands: Horizontal unpainted paper strips bordered by soft ink fades

Water treatment: Flat unpainted paper or faint horizontal ripple lines

5. Ink Gradation Logic

How are the 5-7 ink tones distributed?

Tonal ladder (from darkest to lightest):

Scorched ink (焦墨): Pure black, used for 2%-5% of marks — signatures, key contour lines, foreground details

Dense ink (浓墨): 80%-90% black, foreground mountain masses, 15%-25% of canvas

Heavy ink (重墨): 60%-70% black, mid-ground forms, 20%-30% of canvas

Light ink (淡墨): 30%-40% black, distant mountains, atmospheric layers, 25%-35% of canvas

Clear ink (清墨): 10%-20% black, mist, sky gradients, 10%-20% of canvas

Broken ink (破墨): Wet-into-wet blooms where tones mix on paper

Accumulated ink (积墨): Multiple dry layers building rich darks, 5%-10% of focal areas

Distribution rule: Every image must show visible separation between at least 4 ink tones when viewed at full size.

6. Xuan Paper Materiality

How does paper texture and translucency appear?

Must include ALL of these:

Fiber visibility: Long cellulose fibers, occasional fiber clumps, irregular paper surface (not smooth like cold-press watercolor)

Ink bleeding halos: Every wet ink area shows a soft halo where water spread beyond pigment — this is the signature of raw xuan

Dry-wet boundary contrast: Sharp difference between:

Wet areas: soft edges, blooming, translucent overlaps

Dry areas: crisp edges, visible bristle tracks, opaque

Translucency in overlaps: Where two ink washes overlap, the double-layer area appears darker but still semi-translucent, not solid black

Paper base color variation: Cream/warm-white paper with subtle tone shifts, not pure digital white

Micro-texture: When zoomed to 150%, should see paper grain, not smooth gradient fills

Avoid: Photoshop smooth gradients, vector-clean edges, cold-press watercolor paper texture, digital canvas texture overlays

7. Color Restraint System

What minimal color, if any, punctuates the ink?

Default mode: Pure ink (水墨)

Only black ink + paper white + subtle paper warmth

Maximum 3 tones of gray externally perceived

Optional mode: Light color accents (淡彩)

Primary palette: Indigo (花青), burnt sienna (赭石), rattan yellow (藤黄)

Application rules:

Color used in < 15% of canvas

Applied as translucent washes OVER dried ink structure, not as opaque blocks

Typical uses: Indigo for distant mountain atmospheric perspective, burnt sienna for autumn trees or earthy cliffs, yellow for sunset sky glow

Prohibition: No vivid stone-blue/stone-green (青绿山水 heavy mineral colors), no pure red except signature seal

Seal stamp:

1-2 red cinnabar seals, each 0.5%-1.5% of canvas

Positioned in negative space or composition corner

Square or round, with archaic script

8. Brushstroke Authenticity

What proves this is real brush-on-paper, not digital painting?

Must include at least 4 of these markers:

Bristle separation: Individual brush hairs visible in dry strokes

Pressure variation: Single stroke transitions from thick-wet to thin-dry

Re-load marks: Visible points where artist reloaded brush mid-stroke, creating density shifts

Tremor and hesitation: Slight hand tremor in long lines (not vector-smooth)

Corner accumulation: Ink pooling at stroke endpoints or direction changes

Accidental splatters: Small ink droplets where brush flicked or dripped

Dry-brush skip: Dragged stroke skips across paper texture, leaving broken line

Avoid: Vector paths, Photoshop brush presets, perfectly smooth tapers, symmetrical splatters

9. Emotional Temperature

What should the viewer feel before analyzing technique?

Choose one primary mood:

Monumental stillness: Vast, silent, timeless mountain presence

Wild momentum: Storm-gathering clouds, wind-bent trees, untamed nature

Misty reverie: Half-seen peaks, veiled depths, poetic ambiguity

Archaic power: Ancient cliffs, weathered stone, geological time

Mood must align with: Ink density (heavy = power, light = reverie), composition (vertical = monumental, diagonal = momentum)

10. Hard Avoids

What destroys the style integrity?

Western painting intrusions:

Linear perspective vanishing points

Chiaroscuro light-shadow modeling

Photorealistic detail rendering

Impasto texture or oil-paint look

Digital artifacts:

Photoshop layer blend modes

Gaussian blur for mist

Perfect circular gradients

Copy-paste repeated elements

Digitally clean edges

Wrong Chinese painting styles:

Gongbi (工笔) fine-line colored illustrations

Literati sparse bamboo-rock minimalism with 70% void

Decorative folk painting flatness

Modern ink-cartoon or ink-illustration

Compositional mistakes:

Centered single subject with vast emptiness

Symmetrical balance

Modern landscape photography composition

Cute/whimsical mood

Variation Engine

Before writing each prompt, select one option from each category to ensure diversity:

Composition Architecture

Ascending peaks: Vertical march of 5-7 mountain layers from bottom to top

Cloud-gate reveal: Horizontal cloud band bisecting canvas, peaks above and below

Diagonal torrent: Massive ink-wet mountain surge from corner to corner

Valley enclosure: Mountains form walls on three sides, opening on fourth

Waterfall dominant: Vertical water-thread commands center, mountains frame sides

Pine-cliff-mist trinity: Foreground pine cluster, mid-ground cliff, background mist mountains

Ink Technique Focus

Wet-dominant splashed ink: 50%-70% of canvas in active wet washes with blooms

Dry-wet contrast: 40% dry-brush texture vs 40% wet washes with 20% transitional

Accumulated ink depth: 3-5 layers of dried ink building rich focal darks

Broken ink fusion: Extensive wet-into-wet where tones merge organically

Structural texture emphasis: Prominent 皴法 texture strokes define form language

Scale & Detail Strategy

Epic wide: Panoramic mountain range, 8-12 peaks visible, sense of distance

Monumental close: Single massive mountain occupying 70%-85% of frame

Layered depth: 4-6 distinct depth planes with clear atmospheric fading

Intimate gorge: Narrow vertical composition, viewer inside mountain corridor

Atmospheric Condition

Post-rain clarity: Deep wet ink, some mist, strong tonal contrast

Rising mist: Lower 40% clear, upper 60% dissolving into vapor

Storm gathering: Heavy dark accumulation in upper canvas, luminous void below

Dawn/dusk glow: Faint warm tone in sky area, silhouetted peaks

Signature Element (choose 1-2)

Waterfall descending 30%-60% of height

Ancient pine with complex branch structure

Stone bridge or pavilion for scale

Lone boat on water surface

Calligraphic inscription block (5-15 characters, vertical)

Mountain temple or hermitage

Winding path or stone steps

Prompt Compilation Process

Step 1: Parse User Input

Extract:

Theme/subject: What landscape concept or mood?

Specific elements: Any requested features (waterfall, pine, mist, etc.)?

Emotional direction: Powerful? Serene? Wild?

Text inclusion: Any calligraphy or inscription desired?

Step 2: Select Variation Recipe

Choose from Variation Engine:

Composition architecture

Ink technique focus

Scale strategy

Atmospheric condition

1-2 signature elements

Diversity check: If last 2 outputs used "vertical layered peaks + wet-dominant ink," choose different options.

Step 3: Write Structured Prompt

Use this 5-paragraph structure:

Paragraph 1: Canvas & Material Base

Paper type, format, texture, base color

Example: "Vertical 3:4 raw xuan paper scroll, cream-white base with visible long fibers and irregular texture, matte absorbent surface, natural paper mottling, scanned flat with no frame."

Paragraph 2: Composition & Spatial Structure

Chosen composition architecture

Coverage density (80%-95% rule)

Strategic void placement (cloud banks, water, sky)

Scale markers

Example: "Five horizontal mountain layers stacking vertically from bottom to top, each progressively lighter in tone, occupying 90% of canvas. Narrow horizontal cloud band at 40% height serving as only major void. Tiny pavilion on foreground cliff for scale."

Paragraph 3: Ink System & Brushwork

3-5 specific techniques from Ink Technique System

Tonal distribution (which zones get which ink tones)

Brushstroke types for different areas

Example: "Foreground peaks in dense-to-scorched ink (80%-100% black) with bold axe-cut texture strokes and dry-brush ridgelines showing bristle separation. Mid-ground in heavy ink (60% black) with large wet washes bleeding into paper fibers. Background in light-to-clear ink (20%-30% black) dissolving into mist. Prominent wet-into-wet blooms where mountain forms merge."

Paragraph 4: Material Authenticity

Xuan paper bleeding halos

Translucency in overlaps

Dry-wet boundary contrast

Brushstroke authenticity markers (choose 3-4 from §8)

Example: "Soft bleeding halos around all wet ink areas where water spread into paper. Semi-translucent overlaps where wash layers stack. Sharp contrast between wet soft-edge zones and dry crisp-edge areas with visible bristle tracks. Slight hand tremor in long contour lines, accidental ink splatters near heavy-load areas."

Paragraph 5: Mood, Color, Avoids

Emotional temperature

Color decision (pure ink or light color accent with specific hue and placement)

Signature seal

Hard avoid list (compressed)

Example: "Monumental stillness, timeless geological presence. Pure water-ink with no color except one red square seal in upper-left void. Avoid: linear perspective, photoshop gradients, digital clean edges, symmetrical composition, decorative flatness, literati minimalism."

Step 4: Generate Image

Use built-in image generation by default

Inspect result at 100% and 150% zoom

Quality check: Can you see paper fibers? Is there visible ink tone separation? Does coverage meet 80%-95% rule?

If result is too sparse, too clean-edged, or too digital, regenerate with stronger material language

## Advanced: User Override Options

If user requests:

**"More empty space"**: Reduce coverage to 60%-75%, but maintain monumentality through single massive form

**"Add color"**: Activate light color accent mode, specify which hue and where (< 15% coverage rule still applies)

**"More detailed"**: Shift toward accumulated-ink technique, add gongbi-style细笔 detailing in 10%-20% of focal area while keeping splashed-ink foundation

**"Simpler"**: Reduce to 3-4 mountain layers, emphasize wet-wash simplicity, minimal texture strokes

**"Modern feel"**: Allow stronger compositional geometry, bolder contrast, reduce traditional signature elements, but maintain paper materiality

**"Include [specific object]"**: Integrate requested element (temple, boat, figure, specific tree) as scale marker or focal accent, ensure it doesn't dominate unless user specifies
